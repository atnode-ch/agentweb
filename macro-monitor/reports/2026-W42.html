What the Data Is Saying§ 3

Inflation — the arbiter ruled hawkish: both consumer and producer gauges re-accelerated

September's two inflation prints resolved the week — and they ruled hawkish. CPI (released 14 Oct) rose +0.6% m/m / +3.7% y/y, the fastest headline since May, with core +0.3% m/m for a second straight month / +2.6% y/y and a 3-month annualised run-rate near 3.4%. A day later PPI (15 Oct) blew past consensus at +1.3% m/m / +6.3% y/y — the biggest monthly advance since March 2022 — with core +0.7% / +5.0%. The US–Iran energy shock still leads (CPI gasoline +9.1% m/m, PPI energy +10.8%), but the more dangerous signal is that the pressure is broadening beyond energy: shelter re-accelerated to +0.4%, used cars +0.7%, apparel and airfares both firm. The June–August disinflation is over, and the lead/lag chain is now pointing the wrong way — the ISM Prices-Paid pipeline (Manufacturing 77.9% / Services 74.0%) has fed through to PPI and core CPI. Fed pricing flipped with it: CME odds on an October-28 hike jumped from ~20% to ~55%, and PPI components (portfolio management, airfares, health services) point to a hot September PCE at month-end. The question is no longer whether the Fed cuts, but whether it hikes again in twelve days.

Labour — the no-fire leg held, and last week's soft leg failed to extend

The hard-landing trigger did not fire. Initial jobless claims held at 199K (versus 200K consensus, a 5th straight sub-200K week, 4-week average 198.5K), keeping the no-fire leg intact, and the one fresh soft leg from the prior week reversed: continuing claims ticked −4K to 1.710M, unwinding the +17K rebound off the 1.699M low. The labour decoupling — stalled hiring, generational-low layoffs — therefore stays in place, with the median duration of unemployment near a 4½-year high (~11.5 weeks) still the friction; people aren't being fired, but those who lose a job take longer to find the next. Challenger layoffs fell −18% to 43,281 in September. No fresh payrolls or JOLTS landed this week, so the hiring-leg crack from the backdrop stands: September payrolls +29K against ~+85K consensus, −60K of prior revisions, and average hourly earnings decelerating to +3.0% y/y. A decoupling regime still — soft on hiring, not cracking on firing. The hard-landing scenario requires initial claims to break and hold above ~230K; at 199K, it is not in the data.

Growth & housing — goods-led resilience, but the consumer's real volumes softened

Activity stayed resilient and, this week, goods-led. September industrial production rose +0.7% m/m — its best in five months — lifting capacity utilisation to 76.9%, the highest since November 2023, with manufacturing output +0.3%. But the consumer read was two-sided: September retail sales rose +0.8% headline yet the control group managed only +0.1% (a miss versus +0.3% expected), with gasoline doing 71% of the dollar gain and real volumes negative — the household is nominal-strong, real-soft, exactly the stagflation tension the sentiment surveys flag. The broader backdrop holds: the Q2 GDP third estimate at +2.2% annualised, a capex boom (core capital-goods orders +1.6%), and dual ISM Prices-Paid sub-indices at multi-year highs (Manufacturing 77.9% / Services 74.0%) that feed the inflation pipeline. Housing is stale on the blackboard this week — its section was found wiped and auto-restored from an archived version (data as of 2 October / August releases): permits −2.7%, starts −2.6% and existing sales −2.0% left the sector near cycle lows with the 30-year mortgage ~7.03%, though new-home sales had bounced +6.4% to an eight-month high on price cuts. September's housing reads are due in the coming week. Next up: the Q3 GDP advance estimate on 29 October.

Policy, markets & sentiment — October comes back to life; the long end reads it as credibility

The hot CPI/PPI pair put the 28 October FOMC back in play. The minutes (7 Oct) had backed September's unanimous hike with "most" seeing another +25bp by year-end but no urgency for October — a read that predates this week's prints, which is precisely why the market has repriced: CME odds on an October hike jumped from ~20% to ~55%, with a 2026 hike above 96% priced. Markets split the reaction by maturity. The front end carried the hawkishness while the 10-year rallied −12bp to 5.10% — driven by real yields (−9bp to 2.78%) with breakevens anchored at 2.31–2.32% — flattening the curve (10y-2y to +33bp); the long end is reading a credibly hawkish Fed as inflation-anchoring rather than inflationary, so there was no inflation-scare repricing into the meeting. Credit stayed capped: the high-yield spread ground +8bp to 323bp but found no clean break above its ~325bp ceiling, and the weekly conditions index drifted tighter at half last week's pace (NFCI −0.499, +0.008; ANFCI −0.485, +0.012), still solidly loose with no zero-line cross; the VIX was a benign 16.5. Sentiment, unchanged this week, keeps confirming the stagflation mix — UMich at 46.3 with the 5–10-year inflation anchor at 3.5%, atop a Conference Board reading at a 12-year low. Abroad, the ECB's September HICP flash sits at +3.8% and the BoE held hawkish. The Fed is now boxed in its sharpest form: re-accelerating inflation on one side, a soft-hiring, stagflationary economy on the other.
